The answer claims that the centre of mass does not change and justifies this mainly by appealing to conservation of mass, which is not the correct reasoning: total mass staying the same does not imply the centre of mass is unchanged. In reality, the ice’s mass moves from a higher position (block) to a thin layer at the bottom, so the system’s centre of mass shifts downward. The response also ignores the requested discussion of how the relative masses of ice and tray affect the magnitude of this shift. To improve, the student should: (1) state that the centre of mass moves downward because the ice’s mass is redistributed closer to the tray’s base; (2) explain that the tray’s own centre of mass is unchanged; and (3) discuss that the shift is larger when the ice mass is comparable to or larger than the tray’s mass, and smaller (almost negligible) when the tray is much heavier than the ice.
